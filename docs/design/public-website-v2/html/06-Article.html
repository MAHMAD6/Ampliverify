The Engineer's Guide to Prioritizing SEO Fixes

How to score every issue by impact and effort, sequence the work, and stop spending weeks on changes that don't move rankings.

Why Most SEO Backlogs Stall

A typical site audit returns hundreds of issues. Most tools label a large share of them “critical”, so teams either try to fix everything at once or pick whatever looks easiest. Both approaches spend effort without a clear link to results.

The fix is to treat SEO work the way engineering teams treat a backlog: estimate the value of each item, estimate its cost, and work in the order that returns the most value soonest.

Score Impact and Effort

Give every issue two simple scores from 1 to 3:

  • Impact — how many important pages it affects, and how directly it blocks crawling, indexing, relevance or clicks.
  • Effort — how much time it takes, and who is needed: a writer, a marketer or a developer.

Group issues by root cause before scoring. Forty broken links caused by one template are one task, not forty.

i

Rule of thumb: high impact and low effort goes first, every time. Low impact and high effort often never needs doing.

Sequence the Work

Order matters because some fixes unlock others. A sensible sequence for most sites:

  • Crawling and indexing problems — nothing else counts if pages can't be found.
  • Template-level technical issues that affect many pages at once.
  • On-page relevance: titles, headings, meta descriptions and internal links.
  • Content gaps and new pages for keywords you don't yet cover.

Verify, Then Repeat

Record a baseline before each batch of changes, then compare rankings, traffic and visibility over the following weeks. Keep what works, learn from what doesn't, and feed the results into the next round of prioritization.

That loop — audit, prioritize, fix, verify — is what turns a one-off audit into steady, compounding growth.
